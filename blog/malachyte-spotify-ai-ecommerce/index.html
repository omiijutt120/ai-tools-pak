Ex-Spotify engineers raise $10M to bring Spotify-style AI recommendations to online stores

Malachyte e-commerce storefront reordering products in real time based on shopper intent

Published: August 8, 2026, 2026. News date: August 6, 2026. Reviewed by AI Tools Pak Editorial.

The news

Malachyte, a startup founded by ex-Spotify engineers Sidd Motwani, Ian Anderson, and Shivaditya Sinha, announced a $10 million seed round on August 6, 2026. The round was co-led by Bessemer Venture Partners and Gradient, with Harpoon Ventures also participating.

The pitch: bring Spotify-style behavioral intelligence to e-commerce. At Spotify the founders built a two-headed recommendation system called Vector AI; Malachyte applies the same thinking to online stores, predicting shopper intent and next actions in real time.

Why most stores get personalization wrong

The problem Malachyte targets is familiar to anyone who shops online: most stores treat every visitor the same. Personalization, where it exists, leans on purchase history, demographics, and logged-in profiles - which means first-time visitors see a generic storefront even when their behavior is screaming intent.

"A search for 'heavy-duty boot' followed by two clicks on steel-toed boots is enough to move work pants and gloves up the page and push dress shoes down, with no account or history required." - Malachyte

The system reads every signal continuously - hovers, clicks, scrolls, search refinements, add-to-cart actions. Context matters as much as the click: a phone visitor at 11 p.m. arriving from an email link is in a different state of mind than the same person on a laptop mid-morning.

Traction and rollout

Malachyte has been testing since 2024 with more than 20 enterprise customers across travel, grocery, and retail. It went live with Fun.com in fall 2025, and has been generally available to Shopify merchants since June 2026 through a native integration, with an API for larger retailers.

For store owners the appeal is simple: personalization that works on anonymous traffic lifts basket size without requiring users to sign up or log in.

What it means for Pakistan

Pakistani Shopify sellers and DTC brands have a conversion problem this class of tool targets directly: most visitors are first-time and anonymous, so profile-based personalization does nothing for them. Real-time intent AI that reacts to the first few clicks matters more for a Lahore clothing brand than for a brand with a loyal logged-in base - because the anonymous majority is exactly where the revenue is being left on the table.

The tools are still early and priced for Western merchants, so the practical near-term play is to copy the logic, not the license: capture behavioral signals, personalize landing pages for anonymous visitors, and test product ordering per session. A personalization checklist for online stores

You can start applying intent-based thinking today, even before adopting a dedicated tool:

  1. Track behavior on anonymous visitors: clicks, scrolls, search refinements, add-to-cart.
  2. Reorder products dynamically per session instead of showing one static storefront.
  3. Treat context as a signal - device, time of day, and traffic source change intent.
  4. A/B test intent-based reordering against your current layout before committing.
  5. Watch tools like Malachyte on Shopify as they mature and trial native integrations.

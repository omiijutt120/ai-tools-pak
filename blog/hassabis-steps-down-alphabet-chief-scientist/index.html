Google Shake-Up: Demis Hassabis Steps Down as DeepMind CEO to Become Alphabet's Chief Scientist

The man who built AlphaGo and AlphaFold is stepping back: Demis Hassabis is leaving the DeepMind CEO seat to become Alphabet's chief scientist, in a leadership shake-up that signals Google's AI strategy is being pulled closer to headquarters.

Published: August 9, 2026. News date: August 8, 2026. Reviewed by AI Tools Pak Editorial.

The news: a changing of the guard at DeepMind

Demis Hassabis, the Nobel prize-winning co-founder of Google DeepMind, is relinquishing his day-to-day duties as chief executive, The Guardian reported on August 8, 2026. He becomes chair of Google DeepMind while also taking on the role of chief scientist at DeepMind's parent company, Alphabet. Google and Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai announced the changes in a message titled 'The next chapter of our AI momentum.'

Hassabis, 50, will hand operational leadership to Koray Kavukcuoglu, his longstanding colleague, who becomes senior vice-president of DeepMind in a deliberately non-CEO role. Kavukcuoglu is US-based — a detail former executives say matters: the reorganisation pulls London-based DeepMind firmly into the orbit of its Mountain View parent.

Hassabis co-founded DeepMind and sold it to Google in 2014, then oversaw a run of landmark achievements: AlphaGo, which mastered the board game Go; AlphaFold, which predicts the 3D structure of proteins and earned him the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry; and more recently the Gemini model family.

Why it matters: Gemini's stumble is the backdrop

The shake-up lands at an awkward moment for Google's AI ambitions. The Guardian quoted a former Google executive saying it would be 'galling' for Google and Hassabis that the latest Gemini iterations have lost ground to models from rivals Anthropic and OpenAI. One former executive put it bluntly: 'The era of DeepMind as an independent actor is over.'

Hassabis himself framed the move as a focus shift — handing over operations while concentrating on 'the big picture' for AI. But for observers, the reorganisation reads as Google tightening control over its most strategic asset: not just the technology, but the roadmap, the budgets and the release cadence.

What it means for Pakistan

For Pakistani users of Gemini and Google AI plans, the leadership change is unlikely to change your subscription tomorrow — but it is a signal worth tracking. If DeepMind's independence is shrinking, expect Google's AI roadmap to prioritise what serves Google's products: Search, Workspace, Android, cloud. That is broadly good news for everyday users, who get AI where they already work, and for developers on Google Cloud.

The competitive read matters more: with Gemini losing ground to Claude and GPT, Google is likely to answer with aggressive Gemini pricing and feature pushes in the coming quarters. That is a moment for Pakistani buyers to compare plans — Gemini Pro and Google AI Ultra in PKR against ChatGPT and Claude equivalents — because promotional pricing usually appears exactly when a company is trying to reclaim mindshare.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the new head of Google DeepMind?
Koray Kavukcuoglu, a longtime DeepMind colleague of Demis Hassabis, becomes senior vice-president of DeepMind. The role is deliberately non-CEO, which observers read as a sign of DeepMind being integrated more tightly into Alphabet.

What does Demis Hassabis do now?
He becomes chair of Google DeepMind and chief scientist of Alphabet, focusing on long-term AI strategy rather than day-to-day operations.

Will this affect Gemini users?
Not immediately. Plans and prices are unchanged. But the reorganisation signals that Google's AI roadmap will be more tightly aligned with Google's products, which could mean more aggressive Gemini features and pricing as Google fights to regain ground on Anthropic and OpenAI.

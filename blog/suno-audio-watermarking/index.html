Suno starts watermarking AI songs as record-label lawsuits close in

Audio waveform with a watermark overlay representing Suno's new track-marking tools

Published: August 8, 2026. News date: August 6, 2026. Reviewed by AI Tools Pak Editorial.

The news

On August 6, 2026, AI music startup Suno announced a package of safeguards for tracks made on its platform: audio watermarking, content fingerprinting, limits on downloads, and updated community guidelines. The tools are aimed at stopping AI-generated songs from being misused on streaming platforms, including gaming revenue systems.

Suno also signed an agreement with lyrics provider Musixmatch to use its Sentinel system for copyright detection. CEO Mikey Shulman said the watermarking tools are designed to be durable and resistant to tampering without affecting the listening experience.

Why now: a legal storm around Suno

The timing is no accident. Suno is fighting lawsuits from major labels including UMG and Sony coordinated through the RIAA, a German court ruled the company broke copyright rules in a case brought by GEMA, and a class action is pending in Massachusetts. In November 2025, 404 Media reported a data breach that, per Have I Been Pwned, affected 55 million users and exposed scraped data from YouTube, Deezer, and Genius.

Despite the legal pressure, investors have kept backing the company - it raised a $400 million Series D in June. The new safeguards read as a bid to show platforms and courts that Suno takes misuse seriously.

"Designed to be durable and resistant to tampering, without affecting the listening experience." - Mikey Shulman, Suno CEO

What the new safeguards actually do

SafeguardWhat it does
Audio watermarkingMarks tracks made on Suno so they can be traced; resistant to tampering

The company has not said whether it is using Google's Synth ID or a system of its own, and the details of the new download policy have not been published.

What it means for Pakistan

Suno is wildly popular with Pakistani creators making AI-assisted songs, and the same tools that make music also make voices: AI voice cloning is everywhere in local ads, dubs, and content. The watermarking shift matters for two reasons. First, authenticity is becoming a feature - platforms are getting better at identifying synthetic audio, so passing off AI tracks as human recordings is a growing risk. Second, monetization channels are tightening: mass-uploading AI songs to streaming platforms to farm royalties is exactly what the new download policy targets.

Frequently asked questions

Will watermarking change how AI songs sound?
Suno says the tools are designed to be durable and resistant to tampering without affecting the listening experience. The company also says the technology is not meant to judge whether a song is good, meaningful, or sufficiently human.

Does the watermark apply to songs already made on Suno?
Suno has not specified whether existing tracks get retroactively marked. The announcement covers tracks made on its platform, and the company says the tools will be used to prevent misuse on streaming platforms, including gaming revenue systems.

Can I still use Suno for my music channel?
Yes, but the updated community guidelines prohibit deceptive audio presented as real and using a real person's voice or likeness without permission. The download policy also bars mass redistribution on streaming platforms, so monetizing through bulk uploads is now a policy and legal risk.
